# Playtest protocol: does the learning emerge from play?

**Status:** not yet run with people. Automated checks, including a "skimmer" who reads nothing and picks the first reply, prove every floor can be finished without dead ends. They can't tell you whether a person *learns*. Only this protocol can.

## Who

- 5–8 adults who have **never heard of** the Performance Capability framework. Five sessions catch most usability problems; eight give a clearer picture of learning.
- Mix in people who play games and people who don't, and include at least two who manage or train others.
- Don't recruit people who know the project.

## Setup (per person, about 45 minutes)

1. A laptop with a fresh browser profile, or **Settings → Reset all progress**. One session on a phone if you can.
2. Say only: *"This is a game prototype. Play it however you like. Please think out loud: say what you're noticing and why you're doing things. I can't help you while you play."*
3. Don't mention learning, leadership, the framework, Ben, or "the right answer".
4. Ask them to play the **Grand Lobby** and the **Data Vault**. If time allows, continue to the Break Lounge.
5. Record the screen and audio, with consent. Take notes using the sheet below.

## After play: a short interview (10 minutes)

Ask these in order, and don't lead.

1. *"Tell me what happened in the lobby."* Can they retell the story and the people involved?
2. *"Why did you decide what you did about Kai?"* Can they explain their reasoning in their own words?
3. *"If you played it again, what would you do differently, and why?"*
4. *"Was there anything the game was trying to tell you?"* Listen for, in their words: knowing something isn't the same as being able to do it; check what changed before blaming someone; a win for one team can cost another.
5. *"Can you think of a real situation at work or at home that felt like one of these?"* This is the transfer test.
6. *"Did it ever feel like a lesson or a test? When?"*
7. *"What did the medal mean to you? Did you understand why you got it?"*
8. *"Would you play the next floor? Why or why not?"*

## What success looks like

- Most players (at least 4 of 5) start without help, and finish the lobby in 15 minutes or less.
- Most players can explain *why* their decision worked or didn't, using the story's own details (the rig, the night shift, the routing rule), without being prompted.
- At least half name an insight close to the intended one (question 4) **and** connect it to a real situation (question 5).
- Fewer than one in five say it felt like a course or a test, beyond the notebook.
- At least half want to replay or continue.

## Turning findings into changes

- **Confusion about what to do.** Fix the room first: markers, the "Now" line, the first character's opening line. Don't add a tutorial screen.
- **"It felt like a test."** Find which screen and which words, and rewrite them into dialogue or the environment.
- **Right choice, wrong reason.** The feedback for that option, or the evidence, needs to make the cause clearer.
- **Insight named but not connected to real life.** Strengthen the notebook prompt for that floor.
